# Draft: ADR 0117 amendment for issue #1265 (per-name and per-zone bounds on the pools)

Status: draft for the project to append to `docs/adr/0117-concurrency-for-servers.md` as a dated amendment. The accepted text is not edited. Point 5 asks for a decision of the project.

## Amendment (date of acceptance): the pools' bounds per name and per zone (#1265)

Point 1 of the amendment of 2026-10-27 (slice S7 merged with issue #1148) left the bounds per name and per zone of the withdrawn S7 resolver (review R7-10 and R7-13) to a later issue. Issue #1265 puts them on the bounded lookup and connect pools of `spec/24 §4` rule 2, which keep their sizes and their start before `main`:

1. **One lookup for the requests of a name that wait together,** names compared without case. A request for a name whose lookup is queued or running joins it only while another request still waits for it, and waits until its own deadline. A lookup whose requests all gave up takes no new one: it never runs if it has not started, and else runs to its end unseen, while a later request of the name starts its own lookup. So residue never answers a later request (review M1 of #1574). Only requests in flight at the same time join, and a join can make a request faster as well as slower: that is the timing of their tasks or races.
2. **A quarter of a pool per zone,** and at least 1, on both pools, the steps left running after their requests gave up included. The zone is a name's last two labels (not the name without its first label, as S7 designed: a wildcard zone answers at any depth, and `x.y.example.net` must not escape `example.net`'s share by its `y`), the `/24` of an IPv4 address with IPv4-mapped IPv6 addresses included, and the `/48` of another IPv6 address. A connection's zone is that of the request's host, so names of distinct zones that resolve to one unresponsive network are not bounded together. A step of a zone at its share waits in its zone's own line and joins the end of the pool's line when a step of its zone ends; after a prune, zones are refilled in the order their first waiting steps still wanted came. The names under a public suffix of two labels, such as `co.uk`, share one zone: a cost in fairness, not in the bound. A stalled name holds at most its zone's share.
3. **Space.** Lines are pruned of steps whose requests gave up once they double since their last prune, so each holds at most twice what it kept at that prune, or 32: a pool holds O(peak waiting requests + threads) entries, at an amortized O(1) per step, except on the path of a prune, whose refill of the zones it freed in arrival order is a sort: amortized O(log n) per step at worst there. A step that panics fails its request at once with `Unreachable` and keeps its thread and its zone's place.
4. **Not taken from S7:** the 5-second memory of a failed name. It is a cache across requests, whose answer would depend on what earlier code looked up (the history that P8 removes), and the bounds above hold without it.
5. **Residue reaches later requests: an open channel, for the project to decide.** A lookup or a connection left running after a deadline cut its request keeps its pool thread and its zone's place until it returns. At a zone share of k, k such steps, which code without tasks can leave one after the other, delay a later request of their zone, possibly past that request's deadline (k is 1 for every pool of fewer than 8 threads; the default `--io-threads` of 64 gives k = 4 on the lookup pool of 16 and k = 16 on the connect pool of 64), so the moment the cut came, which may depend on what earlier code did, can reach that request's `Ok` or `TimedOut`. Before #1265 that needed a full pool; the channel exists at every share; it is inherent to bounding hung steps, since a residue that did not count in its zone's share would let a stalled zone take every thread. It is runtime state left by earlier code, not out of model (P8, P12), and `spec/26 §7` rule 5 now says so: `L_res` covers residue of cancelled tasks but not a deadline's cut. The outcome it reaches is `internal` (`spec/24 §5`), so the flow is at most from earlier timing into an `internal` value. Options: (a) make `L_res` count every `Http` request a deadline can cut, joining its deadline's timing label (stricter, may reject programs accepted today, which the preview's rule against growing stricter would need the project to allow); (b) accept the channel explicitly, as stated, for the preview; (c) wait for the reactor's non-blocking resolution, which removes the pools. The PR implements none of (a) to (c) and states the channel; accepting (b) is a weaker information-flow guarantee and needs the project's approval.
6. **What stays open besides.** Many distinct zones that all stall (an attacker's own domains, many `/24`s) can still hold every thread of a pool, each request ending at its own deadline.
