# Draft: ADR 0133, Amendment (2026-11-03): the AES S-box as a published Boolean circuit

- Status: Proposed. A draft for issue #1480, written after the project decided on 2026-11-03 that an amendment is to be made. It stays here until the project decides it. Once accepted, it is appended to `docs/adr/0133-own-cryptography-for-tls.md` as a dated amendment, and the accepted text above it stays unchanged.
- Amends: ADR 0133 rule 14 (AES-GCM, the SubBytes design), and with it the AES items of rule 7 (wipe), rule 9 (timing evidence) and rule 10 (measured speed). Rules 5, 6 and 8 and Alternative E are unchanged.
- Source of the proposal: `ops/coordination/perf-scan-2026-11-02/runtime.md`, items RUNTIME-20 (the circuit) and RUNTIME-S6 (the measurement of `tests/perf.rs`).
- Target: `crates/una-crypto` only. No construct of the language changes, so no edition changes. Nothing shipped changes behaviour: AES-GCM computes the same function, and `ring` stays the default provider until slice 7b.

> **In plain words.**
> - **Today.** The AES S-box is computed as the GF(2^8) inverse `x^254` by four bitsliced multiplications and seven squarings, then the affine map: about 900 word operations per call, plus the wipes of its intermediates.
> - **Change.** Compute it with a published, minimized Boolean circuit (Boyar and Peralta, or an equivalent published and verifiable circuit) on the same eight bit planes: only AND, XOR and NOT, a fixed straight-line sequence, on the order of a hundred and a quarter operations.
> - **Same guarantees.** Still bitsliced, still no table indexed by a secret and no secret-dependent branch. Correctness is checked on the whole input domain (all 256 inputs) against the FIPS 197 table and the arithmetic definition.
> - **Expected.** A faster AES-GCM, stated as an expectation until measured; the measurement first needs `tests/perf.rs` to stop timing a 32 MiB allocation.

## A. Rule 14 today, and what changes

Rule 14 says today: "SubBytes computes the inverse in GF(2^8) as `x^254` with a chain of four bitsliced multiplications and seven squarings, then the affine map; ShiftRows and MixColumns are fixed lane permutations; the key schedule uses the same SubBytes. A test checks the bitsliced S-box against its arithmetic definition on all 256 inputs." The code is `crates/una-crypto/src/aes.rs` (`reduce`, `gf_mul`, `gf_square`, `sub_bytes`).

After this amendment, rule 14 reads, for SubBytes:

1. **SubBytes is a published Boolean circuit.** SubBytes is computed on the eight 64-bit planes by a fixed straight-line circuit of AND, XOR and NOT (XNOR written as XOR and NOT) taken from a published minimization of the AES S-box (section B), which includes the affine map. Its body uses no other operator: no shift, no addition, no multiplication, no comparison, no index derived from data, no branch and no loop whose count depends on data. The key schedule uses the same SubBytes.
2. **Where the circuit comes from is recorded.** The module comment names the paper, the figure or listing the circuit was taken from, and its gate count, and states that the code was written from that listing, not copied from another implementation's source (ADR 0133, plain-words summary: "written from the specifications").
3. **Correctness on the whole domain.** Tests check the circuit on all 256 inputs against the S-box table of FIPS 197 (Figure 7), held in test code only, and against the arithmetic definition (the existing test, kept); and on the FIPS 197 known answers, the Wycheproof AES-GCM file and the differential tests against `ring` (section C).
4. **Unchanged.** The state layout (eight planes, four blocks, lane `16 * block + 4 * column + row`), ShiftRows, MixColumns, AddRoundKey, the key schedule's structure, GHASH, the 96-bit nonce and 128-bit tag, ChaCha20-Poly1305 first in every suite, and the prohibition of tables indexed by secrets (rule 6) stay as rule 14 states them.
5. **Withdrawn.** The sentence on the `x^254` chain. The `x^254` code leaves the shipped crate; it MAY stay in `#[cfg(test)]` as a second reference for the differential test.

Rule 10's figures for AES-GCM ("about 55 MiB/s for AES-128-GCM and 45 MiB/s for AES-256-GCM") and the same figures in rule 14 are replaced, in the implementation change, by the measurement of section E, with the machine, the `rustc` version and the commit.

## B. The circuit and its source

**Candidates** (all by Joan Boyar and René Peralta, with co-authors), each a straight-line circuit over GF(2) with a top linear layer, a shared nonlinear middle (the inversion in a tower field) and a bottom linear layer:

1. J. Boyar and R. Peralta, "A new combinational logic minimization technique with applications to cryptology", SEA 2010, LNCS 6049, pp. 178-189; preprint IACR ePrint 2009/191 ("New logic minimization techniques with applications to cryptology"). The authors state it is the smallest-gate-count circuit for the AES S-box known at the time.
2. J. Boyar, P. Matthews and R. Peralta, "Logic minimization techniques with applications to cryptology", Journal of Cryptology 26(2), pp. 280-312, 2013, the journal version of the technique.
3. J. Boyar and R. Peralta, "A depth-16 circuit for the AES S-box", IACR ePrint 2011/332 (published in IFIP SEC 2012). Its abstract states 128 gates and depth 16 (checked on the ePrint page on 2026-11-03).
4. A later Boyar circuit of 113 gates is cited in the literature on S-box circuits and in the scan (RUNTIME-20); its exact reference is not verified in this draft.

**What is not claimed here.** Of the gate counts above, only the 128-gate, depth-16 figure was checked against a primary source while this draft was written. The other counts commonly quoted (115 gates, of which 32 AND, for the SEA 2010 circuit; 113 for the later one) were not checked against the papers, and this draft does not reproduce any circuit's gate list: it cannot do so exactly from memory, and a listing reconstructed from memory must never be used. **The implementation slice takes the circuit from the published listing**, records the paper, the listing and its exact counts of AND, XOR and XNOR gates in the module comment and in the amendment's final text, and verifies it exhaustively (section C). The amendment's final text quotes only counts that the implementation slice read from the paper.

**Recommended choice.** The smallest published gate count among the candidates whose listing is available and verifiable (on present knowledge, the SEA 2010 / Journal of Cryptology circuit or the later 113-gate one). In software on 64-bit planes every gate is one instruction of one cycle's latency, and depth matters only through instruction-level parallelism, which a circuit of over 100 gates on 4 to 8 wide cores already has; so gate count, not depth, decides. The depth-16 circuit is the fallback if the smallest one's listing cannot be obtained in full.

**Prior use.** Bitsliced AES with a Boyar-Peralta S-box is a known constant-time software technique (for instance BearSSL's `aes_ct64`, whose state layout of eight 64-bit planes for four blocks matches this crate's). This is cited as evidence that the approach is established, not as a source: no code is taken from another implementation.

## C. Correctness argument

1. **Lane independence, by construction.** AND, XOR and NOT on `u64` act on each bit position separately. A function whose body uses only those operators on the eight planes computes, in each of the 64 lanes, the same Boolean function of that lane's eight bits, independently of the other lanes. Rule A.1 restricts the body to those operators, and the review checks it (a reading of a straight-line function of about a hundred and a quarter lines).
2. **Exhaustive check.** Given 1, the circuit is correct exactly when it maps each of the 256 bytes correctly in one lane. The test evaluates all 256 inputs, and, beyond what 1 needs, places every input in every one of the 64 lanes (256 x 64 = 16,384 lane evaluations, four packed calls per rotation, negligible time), so a mistake in the argument of 1 (an operator other than AND, XOR and NOT slipping in) would still be caught. The references are (a) the S-box table of FIPS 197 Figure 7, as a `#[cfg(test)]` constant indexed by the test input (public), and (b) the arithmetic definition already in `aes.rs` (inverse by search, then the affine map). With the whole domain covered this is an exhaustive check, which settles the function's correctness for every input; it is still stated as *tested (exhaustively)*, not as proven by the solver.
3. **Known answers and vectors.** FIPS 197 Appendix B and C (the unit tests of `aes.rs`), the vendored Wycheproof `aes_gcm_test.json` with its counted run (rule 11), the test cases of the GCM specification (the unit tests `gcm_spec_test_cases` and `aes256_gcm_spec_test_case_16` of `aes_gcm.rs`), and the differential tests against `ring` in `tests/aes_gcm.rs` (rule 12), unchanged. The provider's suite-by-suite handshake tests against `ring` (`crates/una-http/tests/provider_parts.rs`, `provider_tls.rs`) run `TLS_AES_128_GCM_SHA256` and `TLS_AES_256_GCM_SHA384` over the new S-box.
4. **Key schedule.** `sub_word` calls the same SubBytes, so the FIPS 197 AES-256 vector (Appendix C.3), whose schedule applies SubWord without rotation (`i % nk == 4`), covers both uses.
5. **Differential against the old code.** While the `x^254` code stays in `#[cfg(test)]`, a test compares the two on random full planes (not only on one lane), as a second, independent reference.

## D. Constant-time argument

1. **No secret-dependent memory access.** The circuit reads its eight input planes and writes eight output planes; every intermediate is a local `u64`. No table, no index from data. Rule 6 (tables indexed by secrets forbidden) is met more simply than today: the current `reduce` indexes a fixed array by loop counters, the circuit has no array index at all.
2. **No secret-dependent branch or variable-time instruction.** AND, XOR and NOT are single-cycle, data-independent instructions on every core Una targets, and on any core: unlike the field multiplications of rule 8, the S-box does not rely on a constant-time multiplier. The one branch of today's affine map is on a public constant (`(0x63 >> i) & 1`); the circuit replaces it by XNOR gates at fixed positions.
3. **What the compiler might do.** Rust and LLVM give no constant-time guarantee (rule 9). A straight-line bitwise function gives the optimizer nothing to turn into a branch, but this is checked, not assumed: the disassembly reading of exit criterion 3 of slice 8 ("the AES S-box") is made on the circuit, recorded with the `rustc` and LLVM versions, on both shipped targets.
4. **The timing test.** `tests/timing.rs` already measures AES-GCM with the key schedule, the hash subkey, the key stream and the tag inside the measured closure (`aes_gcm_time_does_not_depend_on_the_key_or_the_plaintext`, AES-128 and AES-256) and the tag rejection (`aes_gcm_rejection_time_does_not_depend_on_the_tag_byte_that_differs`). It is rerun unchanged after the circuit lands, with `cargo test --release -p una-crypto --test timing -- --ignored --test-threads=1 --nocapture` so the `t` statistic is in the log: once on the development machine when the implementation change is built (a light run, part of its evidence), and as release evidence on the release candidate, on a quiet machine, on AArch64 and x86-64 (exit criterion 2 of slice 8). The pass condition stays `|t| < 10`. One test is added: the S-box alone (a packed call on a fixed plane set against random plane sets), so a leak in SubBytes is not diluted by GHASH and the key schedule.
5. **Wipe (rule 7).** Rule 7 lists "the powers of the state and the product temporaries of the AES S-box" among what is wiped. With the circuit those named arrays disappear; the intermediates are about a hundred `u64` locals, which the compiler keeps in registers and spills to the stack. Rule 7 already states that safe Rust cannot wipe register and stack copies of by-value temporaries. Two forms are possible (Question 2): (a) the circuit's intermediates live in one fixed scratch array of the call, wiped with `Zeroize` when SubBytes returns (the spill slots the compiler chooses may still differ from the array, so this is a best effort, at a cost of one volatile store per intermediate per call); (b) the intermediates are plain locals under rule 7's stated limit, and only the input and output planes are wiped, as `encrypt4` does today. Either way the state planes and round keys stay wiped as now.

## E. Expected gain, and the measurement that confirms it

**First, the measurement must be fixed (RUNTIME-S6).** In `tests/perf.rs`, `run` does `let mut buf = m.to_vec()` inside the timed closure (the AES-GCM arm and the ChaCha20-Poly1305 arm), so each timed run of the 32 MiB case includes a 32 MiB allocation, its page faults and the copy. That distorts both the throughput and the 8x linearity ratio. The fix, a slice of its own before the circuit:

1. allocate each buffer once outside the timed closure and seal it in place repeatedly (the cost of a constant-time AEAD does not depend on the bytes it holds, so no refill is needed; if one is kept, it is a `copy_from_slice` into an existing buffer, and its share is stated);
2. add record-sized cases, 64 B, 1 KiB and 16 KiB (the TLS maximum record), since the provider encrypts records, not 32 MiB bodies, and a separate case for key setup (`AesGcm::new_128` and `new_256`: ten or fourteen `sub_word` calls, each a full SubBytes, plus one `encrypt4` for the hash subkey);
3. print the MiB/s and the per-record and per-setup times with `eprintln!` so the run's log is the evidence, and keep the assertions loose (linear ratio, floors an order of magnitude below the measurement) as the file's comment requires on a shared machine.

**Baseline, then change.** With the fixed `perf.rs`, the current S-box is measured (the baseline), then the circuit, on the same machine, quiet, `--release`, `--test-threads=1`, same `rustc`, best of several runs, with commit and toolchain recorded.

**Expectation (an estimate from counting operations in the code, not a measurement).** Today one SubBytes call is four `gf_mul` (64 AND and 64 XOR each, then a 28-XOR reduction), seven `gf_square` (a 28-XOR reduction each) and the affine map (about 40 operations): roughly 850 to 900 word operations, plus the volatile stores of the wipes (`reduce` wipes its 15-word array on each of its eleven calls, and `sub_bytes` its seven named arrays). The circuit is on the order of 115 to 130 operations. A round also spends about 300 operations in ShiftRows, MixColumns and AddRoundKey, and each `encrypt4` about 4,000 in `pack` and `unpack`. So for AES-128, `encrypt4` would drop from roughly 18,000 to roughly 8,000 operations: **about 2x on the block cipher**, less on AES-GCM because GHASH does not change. The scan's estimate of 2x to 3x on AES-GCM is the upper end. The figure that goes into rule 10 is the measurement, not this estimate; if the measured gain is below 1.3x on the 16 KiB record case, the change is still worth keeping only if it is not slower anywhere, and the result is reported as it is.

Faster `pack`/`unpack` (RUNTIME-27, a bit transposition) and the tag mask in the first batch (RUNTIME-25) are separate changes that need no amendment, since they keep rule 14's text; after this circuit, `pack`/`unpack` become the largest share of `encrypt4`.

## F. Complexity and code size

- Complexity unchanged: `O(1)` per four blocks, `O(n)` for `n` bytes of stream, key setup `O(1)`.
- Code: the circuit is about 115 to 130 single-operation lines (one `let` per gate) in `sub_bytes`; `reduce`, `gf_mul` and `gf_square` (about 30 lines) leave the shipped code, or move to `#[cfg(test)]` as the reference. A net growth of the order of 80 to 100 lines in `aes.rs`, in a function that is easier to review than it is long, since each line is one gate checked against the paper's listing. Machine code: straight-line, no loops; somewhat larger than the current loops, with no table in data.

## G. Risks

1. **A wrong gate.** Caught by the exhaustive test (C.2), which covers the whole domain; a transcription error cannot survive it.
2. **A wrong or misattributed source.** The circuit is taken from the published listing, its reference and counts are recorded, and the counts quoted in the final amendment are read from the paper (section B). If no complete listing can be obtained, the change does not go ahead with a reconstructed one.
3. **The optimizer.** A straight-line bitwise function is the case LLVM is least likely to turn into branches; the disassembly reading (D.3) and the timing test (D.4) are the checks.
4. **Register pressure and spills.** About a hundred live values exceed the register file, so some spill to the stack: a performance effect, measured, and a wipe question (D.5, Question 2), not a timing one (spill addresses are fixed, not data-dependent).
5. **Review burden of slice 8.** If this lands before slice 8, slice 8's review and disassembly reading cover the final code once. If it lands after, those items of slice 8 are redone for `aes.rs`. Landing it before slice 8 is recommended.
6. **Licence.** A circuit is a mathematical description taken from a paper; no code of another implementation is copied, so no licence is brought into the crate.

## H. Implementation steps, as slices

1. **S1, the measurement (RUNTIME-S6).** Fix `tests/perf.rs` as in section E (allocation outside the timed closure, record sizes, key setup, printed figures). Record the baseline of the current S-box. Its own pull request; no rule text changes.
2. **S2, the reference tests.** Add the FIPS 197 table test over all 256 inputs in every lane, against the current `x^254` code, and the S-box-only timing test (D.4). They pass on today's code, so they are known to test the right thing before the circuit exists. May merge with S1.
3. **S3, the circuit.** Take the circuit from the chosen paper's listing (section B), write `sub_bytes` from it under rule A.1, move the `x^254` code to `#[cfg(test)]`, add the differential test of C.5, and apply the wipe form the project chooses (Question 2). Evidence: the exhaustive tests, FIPS 197, Wycheproof, the differential tests against `ring`, the provider tests with `--features own-provider`, `perf.rs` against the S1 baseline, and one run of `tests/timing.rs` on the development machine. Adversarial review of the change (it touches the trusted base of slice 8).
4. **S4, the text.** This amendment appended to ADR 0133 with the final paper reference, the exact gate counts and the measured figures in place of the 55 and 45 MiB/s. (`docs/security/tcb.md` does not name the S-box design today, so it needs no change for this.) May be the same pull request as S3.
5. **On the release candidate (slice 8, unchanged in kind):** the timing tests on both targets and the disassembly reading of the new S-box, as exit criteria 2 and 3 already require.

## I. Questions for the project

1. **Which circuit.** The smallest published gate count whose listing is available and verifiable (recommended), or the depth-16, 128-gate circuit (ePrint 2011/332)? The choice is made in S3 from what the papers actually list; this question only fixes the rule for choosing.
2. **Wipe of the circuit's intermediates (D.5).** (a) a scratch array of the call wiped with `Zeroize` (more conservative, a measured cost), or (b) plain locals under rule 7's stated limit for by-value temporaries. Recommended: (a), following the rule of always choosing the more secure option, unless S3's measurement shows its cost removes most of the gain, in which case S3 reports both figures and the project decides.
3. **Order against slice 8.** Land S1 to S4 before slice 8's review and disassembly reading (recommended, section G.5), or after, redoing those items for `aes.rs`?
4. **S1 as its own card.** The `perf.rs` fix also serves the ChaCha20 item (RUNTIME-19) and the other crypto items of the scan; recommended as a card of its own that this card depends on.
