# ADR 0123 amendment draft: the fault policy as implemented (decision 10 of the S4 amendment, #1391)

Status: Proposed (draft for the project's decision; it is appended to `docs/adr/0123-http-servers.md` only once accepted, with the date of its acceptance). The accepted text of ADR 0123 and of its amendment of 2026-11-02 is unchanged. Decision 10 of that amendment ("Faults: contain per connection, stop the run in three cases", decided on 2026-11-02) asked #1391 to fix the threshold, to state the error of a stop, to close three windows and to amend `spec/31 §6` rule 13. #1391 implements the policy in `crates/una-http/src/serve` (`fault.rs`) and amends `spec/31 §6` rule 13 with it. This draft records what the implementation chose where decision 10 left a choice, so that the record states it; nothing in it weakens decision 10.

## Amendment (proposed 2026-11-04): the fault policy as implemented (#1391)

Decision 10 of the amendment of 2026-11-02 reads as follows, the words "to be implemented by #1391, open" and "Today's code, from #1376, ... changes it to" no longer applying:

1. **The threshold.** The run stops when its contained faults pass 64: the 65th stops it. The count is the run's, over every grant together, as decision 10 writes ("the contained faults of the run"); the card #1391 wrote "the faults of a grant ... 64 per run", and the run's count is the stricter reading, since it never stops later than a grant's would and equals it with one grant. The value is a constant of the host (`FAULT_LIMIT`), the same for every run, and not a setting. Each grant also keeps its own count, for `faults()` at S5.
2. **What counts.** A contained fault is a panic caught in a connection's step, in the closing of a connection (each faulting step of a resumed close counts once), in the hand-off of an accepted connection (from the slot taken before `accept` to the connection's place in its owner's table), in a peer's expiry, and in the giving back of an ended thread. A callback's panic is not a contained fault: it stops the run by itself. A serve thread that a panic ends is counted apart (`threads_ended`), and does not count toward the threshold: there are at most `--serve-threads` of them.
3. **A lock of a grant's shared state** is one of the locks of item 7 of section B and of decision 4 that the host holds: a delivery queue, the request slot of an exchange, a connection's pending reply, the pressure heaps, a registry shard, and a shard of the peer table. Each is taken through one guard that records a panic that begins while the guard lives, and a mutex already poisoned when it is taken; the other locks of the engine (an ended thread's inbox, a listener, a thread's own registrations) are not grant state. A panic that began before the lock was taken, such as an exchange dropped while the program's task unwinds, whose drop answers `500` under the reply lock, does not count, as for the poisoning of a standard mutex. A panic with such a lock held is still contained where it happens (it closes that connection), and the run then stops. One step runs with such a lock held and changes nothing it protects: the socket's write of a connection's pending reply, under the reply lock. Its panic is caught inside the lock, the lock is released, and the panic is raised again outside it, so it is contained to that connection and does not stop the run, and it is quiet. With TLS (#1059) the write processes what the peer sent, so a stop there would be a peer's lever on the whole server; the card #1059 carries this as a precondition (the TLS write keeps that shape, or runs outside the lock). Decision 4's pressure accounting is unchanged.
4. **The stop.** A stop is the hard stop of `spec/31 §6` rule 9, made once. The guard of a lock only records the failure, since it runs with the lock held and the hard stop runs the callback and sends to every thread; the stop is made at the next point that holds no lock: where the fault was contained, at the top of every serve thread's loop, at the next delivery and at the next reply. From the failure on, nothing is accepted (a refusal `Failed` at accept), a reply is discarded as for a departed peer, and the next delivery reports the failure. The listeners close at the first serve thread loop that sees it. A failure recorded on the program's side (a delivery or a reply that panics under a grant lock) wakes every serve thread as it unwinds, by one byte to each wake pair, which cannot panic, so the threads enforce the stop without waiting for an event. A reply checks for the failure before it is queued: one that passed the check just before the failure was recorded may still be queued, and the hard stop closes its connection; the stop is lock-free, so this is its linearization point.
5. **The error of a stop.** The host's failure names its cause: `the serve engine stopped the run: a serve callback panicked`, `the serve engine stopped the run: a panic left the shared state of a grant inconsistent`, or `the serve engine stopped the run: more than 64 faults were contained`. When ADR 0123 step 5 (#1571) wires the engines to a program, the run aborts with that message as U8001, an internal error of the host, exit code 3 (`spec/11 §4`) and the status `aborted`: every one of the three is a fault of host code, since no Una code runs on a serve thread and the callbacks S5 registers are the host's. No new diagnostic code is needed.
6. **A callback that panicked** is not called again once its panic is seen, so a broken callback prints once per serve thread at most, not once per connection the stop closes; calls already under way on other serve threads finish. The other callback is still called, so a program waiting in `next` is woken and sees the failure.
7. **Quiet containment.** A contained step runs under a thread-local mark, and a hook chained once to the process's panic hook passes over a panic made under it. A panic is printed, inside a contained step too, when it happens in a callback, with a lock of a grant's shared state held, or inside a serve thread's timers or readiness registrations. A program that sets its own panic hook later keeps the filter only if it chains to the hook in place, as the runtime's own hook does. Each install takes the hook in place and sets its own, which is not atomic: the serve engine's and the runtime's (`una-rt`) installs must not run at once on two threads, or one hook is lost. Today each runs on its own start path; S5 (#1571), which starts both in one process, installs both from one place before any thread starts (a precondition noted on #1571).
8. **The windows.** (a) The admission records the peer's place as soon as it is taken, so a fault before the handshake checks gives it back; (b) the slot and the place pass to the hand-off message only once the message is in the owner's inbox, so a fault before gives both back, and an owner that ended meanwhile closes the connection at the sender, as before; (c) the owner puts a connection in its table before any step, and a fault before that closes it and gives back its slot and place; (d) the close marks each of its steps done before it runs (the timer, the exchange, the body, the waiting place, the pending reply, the peer's place, the slot, the wake of the program's side, the readiness) and is resumed after a fault, at most eight times, more than its steps, so a fault that repeats at the same point passes it, every step after it still runs, and none runs twice. Window (c) was not named in decision 10; it is the same window on the owner's side.
9. **Costs.** O(1) per contained fault and per lock taken (two thread-local counters and one flag); the stop is the hard stop's O(connections + queued requests).
10. **At S5 (#1571).** `faults()` reads the counts of item 1, and each contained fault is a trace event: both are written at the one place every contained fault passes (`Shared::contain`, with the site of item 2), which S5 extends.
