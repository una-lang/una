# U2037: A decimal scale out of place

In edition `0.5` (preview), a decimal scale is in a place where it would not fit.

## Why Una rejects it

A `Decimal[s]` holds an exact number with s digits after the point, and its scale is part of the type (`spec/29 §16`, ADR 0120). The scale is an integer literal from 0 to 38. A value never loses a digit silently, so the checker refuses every place where a scale would not fit:

- a scale above 38, or a `Decimal` type without its scale in brackets;
- a float literal with a nonzero digit beyond the scale it takes, such as `19.995` as a `Decimal[2]`, also as the other operand of `+`, `-` or `*`, where the literal takes that operand's scale and never widens it;
- a literal whose coefficient would have more than 4,300 digits, the language's digit cap;
- a product whose scale, the sum of its operands' scales, is above 38;
- `widen` to a smaller scale, and `rescale` or `rescale_exact` to a scale that is not smaller;
- a scale argument, `scale:`, that is not an integer literal.

## How to fix it

- Give the literal its own type (`const fee: Decimal[3] = 0.005`).
- Write fewer digits.
- Rescale an operand first.
- Or use the method that matches the direction of the change: `widen` adds digits, `rescale` drops them with a written rounding mode.
