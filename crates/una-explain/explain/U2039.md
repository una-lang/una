# U2039: A `map`, `zip_map` or `update` in a function with `simd required` whose lambda cannot be vectorized for a listed target

A function of a `systems` package of edition `0.5` may require that its loops use the processor's vector instructions, with the clause `simd required target neon, avx2` after its `ensures` clauses: the `simd required` clause of ADR 0120 rule 58. Every `map`, `zip_map` and `update` with an inline lambda in its body, on a dense value of a float, complex or fixed-width element type, must then be vectorizable for each listed target. A lambda is vectorizable when its parameters, its captured values and its result have raw types (`F64`, `F32`, `I8` to `U64`), and its body uses only `let`, an `if` on a raw condition, literals, and operators and methods that have a vector instruction on each listed target. It calls no function, performs no effect and cannot fail. U2039 names the construct, the target and the reason when one is not.

Vectorization never changes a result: the lambda means the scalar loop it equals, and its checks and obligations stay (ADR 0120 rule 55, ADR 0109 rule 5). The repair is to remove the operation that has no vector instruction from the lambda, such as a call or a float `rem`, to compute it outside the loop, or to drop the target that lacks the instruction from the clause.

The clause lands with its slice of ADR 0120. Until then the release reads no `simd required` clause, so no program reports U2039, and this text has no example of its own.
