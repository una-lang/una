- **`PriorityQueue` in edition `0.4`** (#979, ADR 0119 rule 13). `std.collections` exports `lowest_first` and `highest_first`, and a `PriorityQueue[T, P]` has the built-in methods `edit push`, `edit pop`, `edit pop_ranked`, `peek`, `peek_priority` and `items`, and `.len`. `pop` gives the item of the least priority for a queue made by `lowest_first`, of the greatest for one made by `highest_first`, and among equal priorities the one pushed first, so the order of the pops is the same in both engines. `pop_ranked` gives the item with its priority as a `Ranked`. A queue lists its items in the order of the pops, renders as a list, and has no `==`. `push` and `pop` take O(log n) comparisons, amortized, `peek` and `peek_priority` O(1), and `items` O(n log n); updates of a queue held only once happen in place. The priorities are the queue's shape: its top label covers their labels, every read carries it, and a priority above an editable queue's top label is U4001. `push` stores its item and its priority with the `refinement` obligations of refined types. The interpreter runs priority queues as a stable 4-ary heap; the native backend reports them as not supported yet. `spec/28 §8` states the operations, their costs and their labels.
