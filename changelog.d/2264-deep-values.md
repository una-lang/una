- **No accepted program aborts with a stack overflow on a deep Core expression** (#2264, from the third review of #2261, lane S). The cap of 4,096 records, variants and lists that the module constants of one body may hold together (ADR 0126) now counts the parts of a test as elaboration shares them: the `given` and `do` lines are one part, and each `expect` line is its own, each sharing a constant once. The cap of 10,000,000 on the size of what substitution builds counts a constant once in each of those parts, so a constant named in k `expect` lines counts k times; a scalar constant, such as a text, which elaboration copies at every reference, counts at every reference. Three references to a constant of 4,095 records and lists in one `expect` line or one `given` line passed `una check` and then aborted `una test` with a stack overflow; they now run, and a part that names more is U2041. A declaration whose Core IR nests deeper than 131,072 levels (each nested Core expression is one level: each statement, and each intermediate value an expression computes, such as a call, an operator, a record, variant or list) is now U0101 at check time (`spec/02 §9`), measured without recursion. Below that bound, every command runs: `una` runs each command, the checker and the tier 1 check of `una lsp` on threads of reserved stack sized from that bound (1,088 MiB, #2339) instead of the 8 MiB main thread or a 2 MiB thread, and `una test` copies and drops the program on such a thread, not on the evaluator's, whose stack `--max-depth` sizes. A written-out `[[0, 1], ...]` of 13,000 lists used to abort `una test`, and one of 32,000 lists also aborted `una core`, `una core --json` and `una build`; both now run.
