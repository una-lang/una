- **Fix: a wait that a `within` cuts never ends before the `within`'s deadline** (#1355, `spec/27 §5` rule 6, §9 rule 4, §10 rule 3). In native code a request waiting at a limit of its grant, or an `acquire` of `std.limit`, read its covering deadline in whole milliseconds as the run's clock reading plus the time left, each rounded down, so the wait could end with its time-out failure up to 2 ms before the deadline in nanoseconds; the `within` then found its deadline not yet passed and let the failure stand, so the native trace lacked the `timeout` event the interpreter's had. The deadline a wait at a limit reads is now the first whole millisecond at or after it, a request's own time-out on the run's clock is rounded the same way, and in both engines an operation that a covering deadline cut returns only once the run's clock has reached that deadline (on its helper natively, never on a worker); the scheduler tests of turn ends (#1268) now check the end the scheduler sets instead of a turn length measured on the wall clock, so load no longer fails them.
