- **`stop` and `skip` in `for` loops, edition 0.4 preview** (#940, ADR 0116 rule 6, `spec/26 §3`). `stop` ends the innermost loop, `stop when c` does so when `c` holds, and `skip` goes on to the next iteration, each with the loop's state as it stands. They are U2034 outside a loop body or when their loop lies outside the nearest task. A statement after an unconditional `stop` or `skip` in the same block never runs and is U2040, whose repair removes it. Whether a loop stops may not depend on data its body did not start with (U4002), and the rest of a body after a `skip` under a labeled branch runs under that branch's label. Core IR gains `exit_k(b, ...)`, with the tag `next` or `stop`, which both engines and the native backend run, and which `una core --json` exports as the node kind `exit`.
