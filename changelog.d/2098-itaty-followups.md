- **Security: the registry service bounds connections that send no request, refuses a missing timeout and survives every passing `accept` error** (#2098, `una-itaty`, in no release). Follow-ups of the review of #1517. A connection that sent nothing, or a byte now and then, held its slot for the 30 seconds of the head, so a few such connections a second kept every slot busy: a request's first byte now arrives within the first-byte timeout (5 seconds) of the moment the connection starts to be read, and the head so far within that timeout and a second per KiB received, so holding a slot for the 21 seconds the largest head allows costs its 16 KiB. One peer (an IPv4 address or an IPv6 /64) that is not a loopback address holds at most `max_connections_per_peer` slots (64) and is answered 503 past them; that bounds one address, not a client with several. A zero timeout left the socket with none, and a refused timeout was ignored: `Service::new` now refuses a configuration with a zero timeout or a zero bound, and a socket that refuses a timeout is neither read nor written. `serve` stopped on `ENOBUFS`, `EPROTO`, `ENETUNREACH`, `EHOSTUNREACH` and the other passing errors of `accept`: only an unusable listener (`EBADF`, `EINVAL`, `ENOTSOCK`, `EFAULT`) ends it at once now; every other failure is tried again, a failure of one connection after 5 ms at most and the others after a pause that doubles from 5 ms to a second, and `serve` ends with the error once failures that do not pass by themselves went on through 30 seconds of pauses with no connection accepted; `serve` puts its listener in blocking mode. A connection can hold two descriptors, its socket and the file it is sent: the need of 2 x `max_connections` + 16 open descriptors is now documented, for the operator to set.
