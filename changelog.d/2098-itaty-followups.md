- **Security: the registry service bounds idle connections, refuses a missing timeout, survives every passing `accept` error and checks its descriptors** (#2098, `una-itaty`, in no release). Follow-ups of the review of #1517. A connection that sent nothing held its slot for the 30 seconds of the head, so a few idle connections a second kept every slot busy: a request's first byte now arrives within the first-byte timeout (5 seconds) of the acceptance, and one peer (an IPv4 address or an IPv6 /64) holds at most `max_connections_per_peer` slots (64) and is answered 503 past them. A zero timeout left the socket with none, and a refused timeout was ignored: `Service::new` now refuses a configuration with a zero timeout or a zero bound, and a socket that refuses a timeout is neither read nor written. `serve` stopped on `ENOBUFS`, `EPROTO`, `ENETUNREACH`, `EHOSTUNREACH` and the other passing errors of `accept`: only an unusable listener (`EBADF`, `EINVAL`, `ENOTSOCK`, `EFAULT`) ends it now, and every other failure is tried again after a pause that doubles from 5 ms to a second; `serve` puts its listener in blocking mode. A connection can hold two descriptors, so `serve` refuses to start under a limit of open descriptors below 2 x `max_connections` + 16, naming both numbers.
