- **Serve thread faults** (#1376, after #1375, ADR 0123 step 4, `spec/31 §6` rule 13), edition 0.4 preview. A panic in a connection's step, in the hand-off of an accepted connection, in a peer's expiry, or in a callback registered on the serve threads, is caught and counted: it closes that connection at most (at accept, its slot and its peer's place come back as far as recorded) and never ends a serve thread. A panic inside a thread's timing wheel or readiness registrations, which every connection of the thread shares, is not contained and ends the thread, also when it happens in the close that follows a contained fault. A serve thread that ends closes every connection it owned and gives back their `connections` slots, peers' places, requests, exchanges and replies, as far as their state records them, hands the peers' expiries it was timing to a live thread (so `https` peer entries still expire), hands on the turn to accept and registers again every listener whose event its last wait returned; a message sent to it later is handled by its sender, and a new connection whose keyed owner ended goes to the next live serve thread, or is refused at accept when none is left. A connection whose answer timer cannot be armed no longer leaves a dead entry in a delivery queue. A second registration of `on_ready` or `on_slot_freed` is now the defined error `AlreadyRegistered` in every build instead of stopping a debug build. The turn to accept is recorded before the message that passes it, so the record is never older than the last pass. Contained faults are counted but not yet shown to the program, and each still prints through the panic hook. No program can name `HttpServer` yet (#1007).
