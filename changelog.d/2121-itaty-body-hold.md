- **Security: the registry service bounds a request's body as it bounds its head, and refuses a registration without the operator's token before its body** (#2121, `una-itaty`, in no release). Follow-ups of the review of #2117. A head that declared a body and sent none of it held its connection slot for the 60 seconds of the idle timeout, with no credential: a body is now read under the head's rule, by the same code, with a body's numbers. At every moment the bytes of the body read since the head have arrived within the first-byte timeout (5 seconds; the idle timeout when that is shorter) and a second per 64 KiB of them, within the total the body already had; no read waits past the idle timeout. So a body that does not come gives its slot back after 5 seconds with a 408, and the longest a note's body holds a slot is the 6 seconds that cost its 64 KiB. A body slower than 64 KiB per second was already refused past its first minute; it is now refused once it is past the 5 seconds and the time its bytes earned, so a slow client completes only a small body (a 64 KiB note needs about 11 KiB per second, a 1 MiB publication about 49). A `POST /register` whose head does not carry the operator's token is answered 403 (registration closed) or 401 (credential absent, malformed or wrong) with no byte of its body read; the discarding of a refused request's body, at most 64 KiB for at most a second, now ends with the body the head declared. New function `http::serve_from` serves the connections an `accept` function gives, each counted under the peer address given with it (`serve_with` gives the listener's); the refusal of a peer past `max_connections_per_peer` has its end-to-end test through it. `http::read_request` refuses such a registration before its body too.
