- **Security: the registry service bounds a request's body as it bounds its head, and refuses a registration without the operator's token before its body** (#2121, `una-itaty`, in no release). Follow-ups of the review of #2117. A head that declared a body and sent none of it held its connection slot for the 60 seconds of the idle timeout, with no credential: a body is now read under the head's rule, by the same code, with a body's numbers. At every moment the bytes of the body read since the head have arrived within a first allowance and a second per 64 KiB of them, within the total the body already had; no read waits past the idle timeout. For a note, a registration and a cosignature the first allowance is the first-byte timeout (5 seconds; the idle timeout when that is shorter): a body that does not come gives its slot back after 5 seconds with a 408, the longest a note's body holds a slot is the 6 seconds that cost its 64 KiB, and a slow client completes such a body when it arrives within 5 seconds and the time its bytes earned (a 64 KiB note needs about 11 KiB per second, a 2 KiB cosignature 0.4). A publication keeps the first allowance it had, the idle timeout (60 seconds), since at most two publications are read at once and so cannot take the connection slots: what a publisher needs does not change, a body of `S` bytes arriving at the service within 60 seconds and `S` / 64 KiB seconds, so the largest publication (260 MiB) needs 63.1 KiB per second, an uplink of about 520 kbit per second for 70 minutes, and an uplink of 400 kbit per second publishes up to 12 MiB. A `POST /register` whose head does not carry the operator's token is answered 403 (registration closed) or 401 (credential absent, malformed or wrong) with no byte of its body read; the discarding of a refused request's body, at most 64 KiB for at most a second, now ends with the body the head declared. New function `http::serve_from` serves the connections an `accept` function gives, each counted under the peer address given with it (`serve_with` gives the listener's); the refusal of a peer past `max_connections_per_peer` has its end-to-end test through it. `http::read_request` refuses such a registration before its body too.
