- **A `within`'s cut of a `tasks` statement ends a request of its tasks with `TimedOut` on every path** (#1266, ADR 0116 amendment P9, ADR 0117 rules 3 and 4, `spec/27 §2` rule 4, §5 rule 6 and §7 rule 4). When the deadline of a `within` around a `tasks` statement cut its block, a started task whose request was parked on the reactor was woken as a cancellation and its request ended `interrupted`, while the same request on a helper of the I/O pool ended with its own `TimedOut`, and one still queued for a helper ended `interrupted` too. The outcome at that deadline, which is timing information, so depended on which path ran the request and, on the reactor, on whether the task or the statement saw the deadline first, a scheduling race. The cut now lowers the statement's count to a value of its own, which wakes no wait parked on the reactor, as it wakes no job running on a helper: the wait ends at its own time-out, the same deadline, and a request still queued for a helper is woken at once and fails with `TimedOut`, never sent; an `acquire` of `std.limit` queued the same way fails with `RateLimited`. Every other cancellation, a failure of the block, a race that a task lost or a cancellation from outside, still ends a waiting request `interrupted` at once. The interpreter's outcome, `TimedOut`, is unchanged. These constructs are part of the edition `0.4` preview.
