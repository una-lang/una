- **`tasks` and `start`** (#969, ADR 0117 rules 7, 10, 11, 12 and 13, `spec/27 §7` and `§8`), in edition `0.4`. `tasks [at most n at once]` runs its block in the current task, and each `start` in it starts a task for an expression and goes on, parking at the bound, by default `max(64, M)` for the memory budget `M` in MiB, at most a quarter of `vm.max_map_count` on Linux. The statement ends once its block and every task it started have ended; a block that fails or stops cancels them first, and leaving it by `stop` or `skip` of an enclosing loop waits for them. A started task that runs past `--task-depth` ends alone, with its `aborted` event and one U6003 line on stderr, while the others run on; a host stop reaches the `for ever` of the block, whose `on stop` runs, and its tasks drain. Paths are `p.s<c>.<k>`. Checks: U2035 for a `start` outside the block of a `tasks`, inside a task within it, in a lambda or with a `with` clause; U3029 for a `start` that can fail; U2011, U2023, and U5001 for a bound below 1. Traces are written as they happen, with `--trace <file>` on `una run` too, the file made before the run starts. The interpreter runs started tasks, and the root of a program with `tasks`, on `una-sys` coroutines under a first-in, first-out schedule that switches after 1024 check points; native code follows the same schedule under `--virtual-time`, on one worker, so both engines agree, and refuses `--virtual-time` for an executable built with check elision. Under the real clock a task's turn ends after 10 ms while another waits. `una-sys` coroutine scopes take bodies that borrow what outlives them. The messages of `async` and `await` name `tasks` and `start` in edition `0.4`, and the report's `out_of_model` list gains "waits at limits and at `start`".
