- **Soundness: deadlines armed and fired in nanoseconds, and the timer thread started before `main`** (#1151, ADR 0116 amendment P8 and P13, `spec/27 §1` rule 4 and §5, `spec/26 §2` and §7). In native code a `within`, an iteration of `for ever within` and a tick of the driver are armed on the monotonic clock in nanoseconds, not whole milliseconds, so each lasts exactly `d` from its arming instead of between `d - 1` and `d` milliseconds by the phase that earlier code, possibly on `secret` data, left; the timing wheel counts nanoseconds, its finest slot one nanosecond wide, and the timer thread sleeps until the next expiration, always a live timer's, and fires each timer at its own nanosecond, never at the end of a millisecond (arming, cancelling and firing O(1), at most 10 cascades per timer; removal eager, so cancelled timers leave no state and cause no later wake); the driver's tick wait and the `Http` wait of a `for ever within` deadline end at their exact instant instead of the next millisecond, polling the whole milliseconds and sleeping the rest, under 1 ms and uninterruptible. A program with a `within`, a `for ever` or a task construct starts the timer thread with its workers before `main`, so whether an earlier `within` ran no longer changes the cost of the next one; one that cannot start stops the run there with U6003, and a program without these constructs starts no timer thread. Virtual time and `Clock.now` stay in milliseconds.
