- **The verifier knows the collections of edition `0.4`** (#982, ADR 0119 V1 to V8, `spec/28 §11`). Each collection has its length, and the verifier knows each operation's effect: an item just added or set is a member, the length grows by one exactly when it was not, `get` after `set` gives the value, a stack pushed then popped is the stack before, a push puts its item at its end of a queue, and every read is `None` exactly when its collection is empty. An update at a key keeps every other key's membership, and a map's `get`, in sets, hash sets, hash maps, bit sets and, in edition `0.4`, maps (ADR 0069 amendment): a key that may equal the updated one keeps nothing, so such a goal is refuted, never proven. `union`, `intersection`, `difference` and `is_subset` give their membership and length facts; `same_entries` and `same_items` give equal lengths and nothing about order. `.len` of every collection, `b.size` and `has` of a set, a hash set, a hash map and a bit set are in the refinement language, so `requires queue.len > 0` and `kind Hall = BitSet where it.size == 100` can be written. Each query stays quantifier-free: the frames and set relations are instantiated at the keys the query looks up, O(k · t) facts for k updates and t keys.
