- **The serve threads** (#1006, ADR 0123 step 4, `spec/31 §6`), edition 0.4 preview. The host code for `serve:` grants now accepts, reads, limits, queues and writes: one listening socket per grant, never shared through `SO_REUSEPORT`, registered in one accept poller that one serve thread watches at a time (the turn passes round by a message), so exactly one thread accepts at a time and a listener event wakes one thread, at most 16 connections per event, with a slot of `connections` taken before each `accept`; each connection goes to the serve thread named by a keyed SipHash of its addresses, under a key of the run that nothing shows. A connection reads its first request within `read` of the accept and later ones within `idle` and `read`, waits at most `answer` for its reply, and writes within `write`; `408`, `421` for a `Host` the grant does not name, the host's own `503`, `500`, and `100 Continue` after a body is admitted come from the host, and every refusal drains the peer for at most 64 KiB or one second and closes. Shared counters on their own cache lines hold `connections`, `waiting`, `/once`, a rate kept as one timestamp, and the reserves of request bodies (`buffers`, `peer_buffers`), delivered requests (`delivered`) and replies (`replies`, `peer_replies`), with a table of peers (an IPv4 address or an IPv6 /64) in 64 shards. Replies over `replies` close the slowest connection of the peer furthest over its share, found in O(log c) from heaps built only under pressure. A host stop closes the listeners and idle connections, refuses what is not delivered and drains the rest; the hard stop answers each open exchange once without waiting. `--serve-threads <n>`, from 1 to 256 (by default the number of cores), is taken by `una run` and by executables. The descriptor limit a serving run raises is the sum of its connections, listeners, poller and wake sockets, 256 for `Http`, the `Files` helpers and 64 for the runtime, and the cap on the sockets of `Http` requests leaves the rest to the server. The timing wheel moves from `una-rt` to `una-http`, unchanged. An `https` grant is not served until the TLS provider is decided (#1059). The serve threads are tested by scripted connections (`tests/serve`), loopback sockets and a consumer written in Rust; programs reach them through `HttpServer` (#1571), and `una run` and `una build` serve with them (#1008, #1782).
