- **An `Int` of any size is written as text** (#1223, `spec/11 §7`, ADR 0004 amendment of 2026-11-26, Proposed): interpolation, `to_text`, `to_json` and the Una form of traces, outputs, failures and test reports write an integer of more than 4300 digits in full, in O(M(d) log d) for its d digits, in both engines and with or without `--trace`, where the run used to abort with U6003 (#923). Before converting a large `Int`, or a `Decimal`'s coefficient, a write reserves the bytes of its text and conversion (96 per 64-bit limb, known from the bit length) against the memory budget, a text built of many parts by interpolation, an event or a message reserves each growth of its buffer, and `to_json` checks the budget every 64 KiB of text it copies or writes (which also closes the same gap for long strings); when they do not fit, the run aborts with U6003 at the write instead of reaching the allocator's hard ceiling. An operation whose argument does not fit leaves no event in either engine. Reading keeps its bound: `parse_int`, `to_int` and `parse_json` still refuse more than 4300 digits, so such a text is written and not read back. This replaces the entry of #1550, whose abort no longer exists.
