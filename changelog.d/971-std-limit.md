- **`std.limit`** (#971, ADR 0117 rule 9 and IF5, `spec/27 §10`), in edition `0.4`: rate limits computed in code. The module, read only by a package that imports it, declares the capability `Limit` with `op acquire() -> Unit fails Limited accepts internal`, its failure kind `Limited = | RateLimited`, and the handler `limiter(at_most: Int, per: Int of Millisecond, waiting: Int)`, which the runtime provides: `with @gate = limiter(at_most: 50, per: second, waiting: 200)` installs one bucket of `at_most` tokens that refills evenly over `per`, kept by GCRA over the run's clock as the limits on grants are, shared by every task that uses the binding, the started tasks of a `tasks` statement included. `acquire` takes a token at once or waits in the bucket's queue, served by ticket, within the deadlines of `within` and `for ever within`; it fails with `RateLimited` when `waiting` calls already wait, when a covering deadline passes or a host stop ends the wait, with no token taken; a canceled task's wait ends `interrupted`. Under `--virtual-time` a wait moves the clock and takes no wall time, so both engines agree. Installing it carries the obligations `at_most >= 1`, `per >= 1` and `waiting >= 0` (U5001, U5002); `una check` reports U4001 at the `with` binding, or the `given` line, when the statement it scopes over is timed by data above `internal`, the label `Limited` carries.
