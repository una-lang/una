- **The operations of `HttpServer` in the interpreter, over the serve engine** (#1571, ADR 0123 step 5, `spec/31 §1`), edition 0.4 preview, not yet reachable by `una run`. `next` checks an abort of the run, `close` and the host stop first, in that order, then takes one request from the engine in one atomic step, lets the other ready tasks run while none can be delivered, and waits for the engine only when no task is ready; `reply` checks the holder of the reply right (`NotHeld`) and the reply (`BadReply`, the right kept), and returns `unit` without sending when another transition ended the exchange; `close` closes the listeners once. Reply rights are words of a table shared by both engines (`una_http::serve::rights`), held by one task at a time: `next` gives the right to its caller, `start` moves the rights its captures reach, closures included, which Core IR now names on the `start` node (`rights`, from the checker's capture mark of IF6), and a task's end answers what it still holds, `500` or `503` with `Retry-After: 1`, each with its `unanswered` event. The arguments of the operations of `HttpServer` are written redacted in traces whatever their labels, until the reduced form of ADR 0123 rule 1 lands. Native code refuses a `start` that moves reply rights. The runs of `tests/serve_runs/` drive a server through the real engine from a script of connections. `una run` and `una build` still refuse a program that serves (`spec/31 §1` rule 10).
