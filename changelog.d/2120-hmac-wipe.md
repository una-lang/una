- **`Keys.hmac` wipes its copies of the key** (#2120, from the review of #1481): the HMAC routine of both engines, `una_interp::hmac_sha256`, copied the key to a heap vector and made two more, the key under each pad, and freed the three without a wipe, with the states of its hashes. It now holds the padded key, its two pads (one buffer, changed in place), the hash state and the inner digest in fixed arrays on its stack, with no allocation but the result, and overwrites all of them with zeros before it returns; for a key longer than the block of the hash, the key's digest and the last bytes of the key that the padding copies are in the same arrays. The wipe adds no dependency: `una-interp` and `una-rt` still do not depend on `una-crypto` or `zeroize`, the routine keeps its own SHA-256 state over the compression function of `sha2` (its feature `compress`, a public name only), and the zeros are kept by an optimization barrier, which is best effort and not a volatile write. Not wiped, as before: the working variables of the compression function, copies a compiler makes in registers, the keys in the run's table for the whole run, the key file's text and its decoding at start-up, and the bearer key that the operations of `Http` copy out of the table into the request they send. Results are bit-identical: the seven test cases of RFC 4231 pass on the routine, those whose data is a text through `Keys.hmac` of the native runtime, and the routine equals the former one over keys and data of every length around the block. No behavior of a program changes.
