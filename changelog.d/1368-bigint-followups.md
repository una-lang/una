- **Big integer follow-ups** (#1368, after #1093). Products whose shorter operand has more than 9/16 of the longer one's limbs take Toom-3 from 256 limbs, where Karatsuba was up to 15% slower than the num-bigint baseline; `Int` and `BigUint` operands of at most two limbs divide in 128-bit arithmetic (a 2-limb by 1-limb division was 1.44x to 1.47x the baseline, now 0.355x to 0.665x); reading decimal text keeps Horner's rule up to 14,592 digits (faster from 7,399 to 12,330 digits, but 1.03x instead of 0.95x the baseline at 19,729 digits, sizes only internal callers reach); writing digits divides by radix powers without their zero low limbs, 5% to 10% faster on random values from 128 limbs. Results are unchanged bit for bit. `docs/plan/1093-bigint-design.md` now states the measured ratios, including the cases still slower than num-bigint (powers of ten written as text: 1.01x to 1.40x at the sizes measured inside the 4,300-digit bound, 1.68x to 2.50x from 256 limbs). An allocation of an impossible size reports the layout that was asked for. ADR 0004, 0055 and 0120's sections of #1093 are headed as amendments.
