- **Dense values in edition 0.4, a preview: `Tensor[T]`, `Lu[T]`, the factorizations and the solves** (#987, ADR 0120, `spec/29 §17`). `Tensor[T]` takes `F64` or a fixed-width integer element type and has a rank from 1 to 8. Its shape, index and permutation are list literals, so each call gets the contract of its rank: `tensor(shape: [2, 3], elements: xs)`, `tensor_filled(shape: [...], value: x)`, `t.get(at: [i, j])`, `t.at(at: [...])`, `edit t.set(at: [...], item: x)`, `t.permute(axes: [1, 0])` and `t.reshape(shape: [...])`. Anything else in their place is the new U2038, and so is an axis that is not an integer literal from 0 to 7. `t.rank` and `t.dim(axis: k)` are in the refinement language. Tensors also have `sum`, `sum(axis:)`, `contract(with:, axis:, other:)` (each element an inner product in blocks of 64, so its bits are `multiply`'s), the element-wise methods and operators, and the conversions `to_tensor`, `to_array` and `to_matrix`. On `F64` matrices: `lu()` gives an opaque `Lu[T]`, with `size`, `factors()`, `pivots()` and `solve(rhs:)`, and rendered as `<lu size n>`. `solve`, `solve_array`, `cholesky`, `solve_lower` and `solve_upper` follow the blocked chains of ADR 0120 rules 48 to 50, so the bits are the same in every engine. A factorization and a solve run to their end whatever the values, and the top of their `Option` carries the labels of the elements. `Lu`, `Matrix` and `Tensor` have no constructor (U2001). Verification gains the tensor sorts with their rank, lengths and element count, the shape frame of a tensor, and the shapes that `lu`, `solve` and `cholesky` hold on `Some`. `to_finite` follows with `F32`; native code follows too, and until then `una build` reports a program with a dense value as not compiled yet.
