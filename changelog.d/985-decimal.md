- **`Decimal[s]` in edition 0.4: exact base 10 for money** (#985, ADR 0120 rules 12 to 21, `spec/29 §16`). A decimal is an integer coefficient and a scale from 0 to 38 that is part of its type. A float literal where a decimal is expected is its exact value, never rounded, taking the other operand's scale in `+`, `-` and `*` and its own digits in a comparison. `+` and `-` align scales, `*` adds them, comparisons are by value across scales, and `/` and `mod` are refused with repairs. Every rounding is written: `widen`, `rescale`, `rescale_exact`, `divide`, `remainder`, `to_int` and the prelude's `Rounding` with seven modes. There are also `abs`, `coefficient`, `to_f64`, `n.to_decimal()` on integers, and `x.to_decimal(scale:, rounding:)` on `F64`. The prelude functions are `parse_decimal` and `as_decimal`, under the shared digit cap of 4,300 (`TooLong`), and `json_decimal`. Decimals are `Eq` and `Ord`, key maps, carry labels, run the same in the interpreter and in native code, and verify as their coefficients (ADR 0120 V2), with `division` and `rescale_exact` obligations and counterexamples shown by value. New code U2037 (a decimal scale out of place).
