- **`BitSet` and the conversions in edition `0.4`** (#980, ADR 0119 rules 14 and 16). `std.collections` exports `empty_bit_set(size:)`, which requires a size of at least 0, and a `BitSet` holds integers from 0 to `size - 1`, one bit each: `.size`, `.len`, `has`, `edit add`, `edit remove`, `with`, `without`, `union`, `intersection`, `difference`, `is_subset`, `first`, `last` and `items`. A bit set lists its members in ascending order and renders as `{1, 2, 3}`; two are equal when they have the same size and members. `has`, the updates, `size` and `len` take O(1); the set operations, `first` and `last` O(size / 64), and `items` O(size / 64 + len). `add` and `with` require their item in the range and the set operations operands of one size, `precondition` obligations that the verifier proves from the size `empty_bit_set` gives and every operation keeps; the run-time check stays and aborts with U8001 if a wrong proof lets one fail. `empty_bit_set` checks its words against the memory budget before allocating (U6003). The members and the size are the shape: a bit set made with a labeled size takes its label, `has` carries the item's labels, and an item above an editable bit set's top label is U4001. `std.collections` also exports the conversions `to_stack`, `to_queue` and `to_set`, called with method syntax on a list. The interpreter runs bit sets and the conversions; the native backend reports them as not supported yet. `spec/28 §9` and `§10` state the operations, their costs and their labels.
